Geodynamic Process

Why Are the Himalayas So Earthquake-Prone?

The Himalayas were formed as a result of the ongoing continental collision between the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates. The relentless northward movement of India produces colossal crustal stress along the Himalayan mountain belt.

STEP 01 — CRUSTAL PLATE MOVEMENT

Indian Plate Moves Northward

Approximately 50 million years ago, the Indian Plate closed the ancient Tethys Ocean and slammed into Eurasia. Today, India continues to wedge northward at a brisk rate of ~40 to 50 mm every year.

STEP 02 — CONTINUOUS STRESS ACCUMULATION

Elastic Strain Locked in Fault Systems

Instead of sliding smoothly, friction locks the major fault boundaries (MHT, MBT, MCT, HFF). Around 18 to 21 mm/year of tectonic convergence is compressed directly into the brittle upper 15–20 km of the mountain wedge, bending rocks like a giant subterranean spring.

STEP 03 — CATASTROPHIC ENERGY RELEASE

Sudden Decollement Rupture & Seismic Waves

When the shear stress finally overcomes rock friction, the fault ruptures catastrophically. In seconds, stored energy explodes outward as violent seismic P and S waves, generating M7+ and M8+ mega-earthquakes and devastating mountain communities.

Hazard Vulnerability Assessment

Himalayan Seismic Hazard Zonation

Visual classification of seismic hazard levels based on active tectonic proximity, peak ground acceleration (PGA), and historical shaking intensity.

ZONE V • HIGHEST VULNERABILITY

Very High Hazard

Peak Ground Acceleration > 0.36g | MMI IX+

Areas with active continental collision structures, steep locked ramps, and extreme rupture probability. Capable of generating catastrophic M7.5+ events with violent ground acceleration and total structural collapse in unreinforced masonry.

  • 📍 Kashmir Valley & Muzaffarabad
  • 📍 Kangra & Chamba (Himachal)
  • 📍 Uttarkashi & Chamoli (Uttarakhand)
  • 📍 Central Nepal & Kathmandu Basin
  • 📍 Assam & Northeast India
ZONE IV • HIGH SEISMICITY

High Hazard

Peak Ground Acceleration 0.24g – 0.36g | MMI VIII

Areas bordering the main collision front where destructive earthquakes are frequent. Encompasses the Sub-Himalayan foothill alluvial belt and major high-density urban corridors that experience amplified distal shaking.

  • 📍 Delhi-NCR & Northern Haryana
  • 📍 Jammu Foothills & Punjab
  • 📍 Southern Nepal Terai Plain
  • 📍 Bhutan Sub-Himalayan Foothills
  • 📍 Southern Tibetan Periphery
ZONE III • MODERATE SEISMICITY

Moderate Hazard

Peak Ground Acceleration 0.16g – 0.24g | MMI VII

Transitional zones with comparatively lower but still important seismic hazard. Thick saturated alluvial soils in this zone are prone to liquefaction and long-period wave resonance from distant megaquakes.

  • 📍 Indo-Gangetic Plains (UP & Bihar)
  • 📍 Central Punjab Plain
  • 📍 Interior Tibetan Plateau
  • 📍 Brahmaputra Valley Margins
ZONE II • LOWER HAZARD

Lower Hazard

Peak Ground Acceleration < 0.16g | MMI VI and Below

Relatively stable crystalline craton shield areas further south of the Himalayan frontal thrusts. Shaking is typically minor, though moderate intraplate activity can occasionally register.

  • 📍 Central Peninsular India
  • 📍 Southern Rajasthan Shield
  • 📍 Stable Peninsular Transition

Structural Geology

Major Himalayan Fault Systems

The Himalayan orogen is dissected by several major boundary thrusts and transform shear zones accommodating continental convergence.

BASAL DETACHMENT Slip: 18-21 mm/yr

Main Himalayan Thrust (MHT)

Depth: 5 to 25 km beneath the mountain chain

The master blind continental decollement plane beneath the entire Himalayan wedge. It separates the underthrusting Indian shield from the upper mountain crust. The locked portion along this plane is the source of the largest M8+ megaquakes.

Fault Geometry: Low-angle thrust decollement
Last Mega-Rupture: 2015 Gorkha (M7.8)
Seismic Threat: Maximum (M8.5+ potential)
FRONTAL THRUST Slip: 10-15 mm/yr

Himalayan Frontal Fault (HFF)

Depth: Surface to 10 km (Emergent Thrust)

The southernmost active tectonic boundary where the Siwalik foothill hills abruptly crash into the flat Indo-Gangetic alluvial plain. Represents the surface manifestation of the Main Himalayan Thrust.

Fault Geometry: Shallow-dipping emergent thrust
Surface Ruptures: 1934 Bihar-Nepal / 1950 Assam
Seismic Threat: Critical (Dense plain proximity)
REGIONAL THRUST Slip: 5-10 mm/yr

Main Boundary Thrust (MBT)

Depth: 10 to 25 km beneath Lesser Himalaya

Major regional thrust fault extending over 2,000 km, demarcating the boundary between the sedimentary Siwaliks and the metamorphic rocks of the Lesser Himalaya. Highly seismogenic with frequent moderate-to-large quakes.

Fault Geometry: North-dipping thrust fault
Key Event: 1905 Kangra (M7.8)
Seismic Threat: Very High
CRYSTALLINE SHEAR Slip: 2-6 mm/yr

Main Central Thrust (MCT)

Depth: 15 to 35 km beneath Higher Himalaya

Historic ductile shear zone that carried high-grade metamorphic gneisses and granites of the Higher Himalayas southward over the Lesser Himalaya. Now exhibits intense micro-seismic clusters along its mid-crustal ramp.

Fault Geometry: Ductile-to-brittle shear zone
Seismicity: High microseismicity & ramp quakes
Seismic Threat: High Intermediate Depth
PLATE SUTURE Paleo-Collision Boundary

Indus-Tsangpo Suture Zone

Depth: Upper to Mid Crust (Tibet Border)

The geological suture marking where the ancient Tethys Ocean vanished and the Indian continent collided with Eurasia ~50 million years ago. Contains ophiolites and marine sediments from the consumed ocean floor.

Fault Geometry: Suture line / Ophiolite melange
Current Motion: Strike-slip & Normal faulting
Seismic Threat: Moderate Intraplate
TRANSFORM SHEAR Slip: 19-24 mm/yr

Chaman & Karakoram Faults

Western Syntaxis & Tibetan Extrusion

Major strike-slip fault systems on the flanks of the Indian plate. The Chaman fault accommodates northward shearing in Pakistan/Afghanistan, while the Karakoram fault facilitates the eastward escape of Tibet.

Fault Geometry: Major strike-slip transform
Notable Event: 1935 Quetta / 2005 Kashmir Syntaxis
Seismic Threat: Critical (Fast slip rate)

Life-Saving Protocol

Earthquake Safety & Preparedness

Crucial actionable steps to safeguard your life and family before, during, and after a major Himalayan earthquake.

🏢

Identify Safe Zones in Every Room

Locate sturdy tables, load-bearing desks, or interior walls away from glass windows, exterior walls, and heavy overhead lighting fixtures.

🧰

Prepare a 72-Hour Survival Kit

Assemble water (3 liters/person/day), non-perishable food, flashlights, battery-powered radio, first-aid supplies, whistle, and personal medications.

⚓

Secure Heavy Furniture & Appliances

Anchor tall bookcases, wardrobes, water heaters, and televisions to wall studs using L-brackets and flexible safety straps to prevent toppling.

🗺️

Establish Family Emergency Plan

Designate an out-of-area emergency contact person and choose an open-space outdoor meeting place away from power lines and brick facades.

STEP 01

DROP

Drop onto your hands and knees immediately wherever you are. This lower position protects you from being knocked over by sudden high-acceleration S-waves and allows you to crawl if necessary.

STEP 02

COVER

Take cover under a sturdy table or desk. If no shelter is nearby, crawl next to an interior wall and cover your head and neck with your arms. Stay away from glass windows and hanging shelves.

STEP 03

HOLD ON

Hold onto your shelter with one hand and stay ready to move with it until shaking completely stops. If under no shelter, keep both hands clasped firmly behind your neck.

💡 Special Mountain Hazard Note: If outdoors in Himalayan mountain terrain during shaking, watch out for sudden rockfalls, boulder slides, and steep slope failures. Move to wide open valley bottoms away from riverbanks prone to flash floods.
🩹

Check for Injuries & Render First Aid

Examine yourself and those around you for injuries. Provide basic first aid. Do not move seriously injured individuals unless they are in immediate danger.

🔥

Inspect for Gas Leaks & Fire Hazards

Check for gas odors. If smelled, immediately shut off main valve, open windows, and evacuate outdoors. Do not operate electrical light switches or matches.

🌊

Prepare for Strong Aftershocks

Secondary aftershocks can strike minutes, hours, or days later, collapsing already-weakened masonry structures. Keep helmets and emergency supplies ready.

📻

Listen to Official Emergency Broadcasts

Tune to battery-powered radios or official civil defense disaster channels. Stay off phone lines to preserve cellular bandwidth for search and rescue operations.
